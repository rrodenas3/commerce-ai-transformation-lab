# Project charter

## Mandate

Design and experimentally evaluate a governed AI-assisted workflow for delayed or partially fulfilled orders in a fictional mid-market commerce company.

The project must demonstrate how a transformation leader moves from business outcome to workflow redesign, human accountability, technical delivery, enablement, evaluation, and an evidence-based investment decision.

## Initial business problem

Order exceptions require information and decisions across commerce, order management, warehouse operations, carriers, customer service, inventory, finance, and payments. Missing context, repeated handoffs, inconsistent policies, and unverified actions can delay recovery and erode customer trust.

This is a research hypothesis. The manual baseline will test which coordination problems actually appear in the synthetic workflow before implementation begins.

## Primary outcome

Increase the proportion of eligible exceptions that reach a correct, policy-compliant, and verified recovery within the target time, while preserving human authority over consequential decisions.

## Primary workflow

The first vertical slice covers delayed or partial fulfilment from the first reliable signal through verified customer-recovery communication.

Adjacent conditions such as contradictory carrier data, lost parcels, unavailable replacement inventory, duplicate actions, and high-value refunds are evaluation variants.

## Actors and decision rights

| Role | Accountability |
| --- | --- |
| Executive sponsor / outcome owner | Sets the priority, risk appetite, and investment decision in the fictional organisation. |
| Workflow owner / local Activator | Owns the recurring exception workflow, operating routine, support route, and feedback loop. |
| Transformation leader — Raul Rausell | Selects the value stream, defines outcomes, redesigns work, allocates human and AI tasks, establishes controls, coordinates the build, evaluates evidence, and recommends scale, revision, pause, or stop. |
| Customer-recovery specialist | Reviews evidence, exercises delegated authority, corrects recommendations, and owns customer judgment. |
| Finance or policy approver | Authorises actions outside delegated financial or policy limits. |
| Technical owner | Maintains the workflow, adapters, observability, and recovery mechanisms. |
| Risk and privacy owner | Reviews data, access, harmful outcomes, monitoring, and incident treatment. |

These roles are part of a fictional operating model. Independent reviewers may exercise selected roles during synthetic-case sessions; that does not make them organisational adopters.

## Synthetic systems

- Commerce or order-management system.
- Warehouse-management system.
- Carrier event source.
- Customer-relationship system.
- Inventory service.
- Refund or payment adapter.
- Policy and knowledge source.

All adapters remain synthetic, local, read-only, or dry-run until a later decision explicitly changes the maturity boundary.

## Raul's decision record

The public journey will show which decisions Raul made, the evidence available at the time, the alternatives rejected, and what changed after failure or review. Authorship of code alone is not treated as transformation leadership.

## Non-goals

- Production deployment or live enterprise integration.
- Real personal, customer, employee, supplier, or former-company data.
- Claims of adoption, realised savings, retained revenue, or improved satisfaction.
- A general AI operating system or broad agent platform.
- Multiple lighthouse workflows.
- Autonomous financial or customer-facing action without bounded authority and verification.

## Exit decision

The project ends its first cycle with one of four recommendations: scale the experiment, revise and retest, pause pending missing evidence, or stop because the value-risk case is insufficient.
