# Workshops, Canvases, and Communication Toolkit

## Make the next decision visible

Use only the workshop or canvas needed for the current decision. Completing every template is not transformation.

## 1. Facilitation principles

1. Invite people who perform, own, enable, govern, and are affected by the work.
2. Start with a case or workflow, not a model demonstration.
3. Mark every statement as observed, reported, assumed, proposed, or unresolved.
4. Separate current state from future design.
5. Map exceptions and waiting, not only the happy path.
6. Ask who has authority at every consequential decision.
7. Surface disagreement; do not manufacture consensus.
8. Capture decisions, owners, evidence needed, and stop conditions in the room.
9. Do not expose sensitive data through an unapproved workshop or AI tool.
10. End with the smallest useful next decision—not a vague action list.

## 2. Company-facing narrative

### Twelve-slide conversation structure

| Slide | Message | Visual |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | AI transformation changes work and outcomes, not only tools | strategy → workflow → evidence → decision |
| 2 | The company's pressure/opportunity | outcome tree or value-stream map |

| 4 | Why AI may or may not fit | AI vs simplification/rules/search/automation comparison |
| 5 | Human and AI authority | autonomy ladder and decision matrix |
| 6 | The connected operating model | sponsor, ATO, workflow owner, Activator, platform, controls |
| 7 | Reference architecture | experience, workflow, intelligence, data, action, trust/evidence planes |
| 8 | Use-case portfolio | Enable, Guide, Co-build, Stop |
| 9 | Evidence model | designed → tested → human → pilot → operational → value |
| 10 | Enablement/adoption | awareness → first use → workflow integration → outcome |
| 11 | Stage-gated engagement | discover → design → test → pilot → decide |
| 12 | Decision requested | sponsor, workflow, boundary, participants, next evidence gate |

Do not lead with personal credentials for half the meeting. Establish the decision method, use the Commerce lab as one bounded example, then ask about the company's work.

## 3. Workshop sequence

### Workshop W1 — Executive alignment

**Purpose:** agree why AI-enabled operating change matters and what decision discovery must support.

**Participants:** sponsor, outcome owners, transformation lead, finance/strategy, technology/data, people/change, relevant control leader.

**Agenda:**

1. strategic pressures and non-goals;
2. AI activity already underway;
3. candidate outcomes/value streams;
4. risk appetite and material boundaries;
5. operating-model ownership;
6. select discovery scope and evidence gate.

**Outputs:** mandate draft, outcome map, sponsor/owners, discovery boundary, decision log.

### Workshop W2 — Current workflow reconstruction

**Purpose:** map how recurring work actually occurs.

**Participants:** workflow owner, representative users, upstream/downstream teams, system/knowledge owners, transformation facilitator.

**Agenda:**

1. reconstruct one routine case;
2. reconstruct one ambiguous or failed case;
3. map queues, decisions, systems, handoffs, rework, waiting, action and closure;
4. identify authoritative facts and conflicts;
5. mark reported vs observed evidence;
6. define baseline questions.

**Outputs:** current-state map, case taxonomy, source map, failure hypotheses, observation plan.

### Workshop W3 — Portfolio and lighthouse decision

**Purpose:** qualify opportunities and choose one coherent workflow.

**Participants:** sponsor/outcome owners, workflow owners, users, transformation, architecture/data, enablement, control partners.

**Agenda:**

1. generate opportunities from observed friction;
2. compare non-AI alternatives;
3. apply pass/fail qualification;
4. assess value, feasibility, readiness, consequence;
5. choose Enable/Guide/Co-build/Stop pathways;
6. agree lighthouse, owner, boundary, baseline and next gate.

**Outputs:** portfolio register and lighthouse charter.

### Workshop W4 — Future workflow and authority design

**Purpose:** allocate human/AI work and redesign accountability.

**Agenda:**

1. eliminate/simplify work first;
2. map future tasks and decisions;
3. assign AI behaviour/autonomy per task;
4. define evidence and policy for decisions;
5. define action, approval, verification, exception and appeal;
6. assign workflow, support, technical, data, policy and risk ownership.

**Outputs:** future-state map, authority matrix, requirements, role changes, stop conditions.

### Workshop W5 — Architecture, data, and risk design

**Purpose:** make dependencies and control decisions explicit.

**Agenda:**

1. inventory/classification context;
2. source-of-truth and information contract;
3. model/capability selection;
4. tool/action permissions and verification;
5. privacy/security/legal/risk scenarios;
6. trace, evaluation, incident, change, vendor and exit.

**Outputs:** reference architecture, connection contracts, risk/control record, open decisions.

### Workshop W6 — Evaluation preregistration

**Purpose:** freeze how the design will be judged.

**Agenda:**

1. decision question and variants;
2. case families and splits;
3. oracle/rubric and human review;
4. outcome, quality, risk, human work, reliability, adoption and cost measures;
5. exact-zero failures and thresholds;
6. version, provenance, denominator and claim rules.

**Outputs:** evaluation contract and evidence plan.

### Workshop W7 — Enablement and pilot readiness

**Purpose:** prepare intended users and operating support.

**Agenda:**

1. audience and role capability;
2. user workflow, instructions and realistic practice;
3. manager, Activator, office hours, knowledge and help;
4. communication, transparency, concern and appeal;
5. pilot scope, users, monitoring, stop, fallback and incident;
6. adoption and workforce evidence.

**Outputs:** workflow package, adoption plan, pilot charter, support and communication assets.

### Workshop W8 — Evidence and investment review

**Purpose:** decide expand, revise, pause, or stop.

**Agenda:**

1. revisit decision and preregistered criteria;
2. review outcome, quality, risk, human work, adoption, reliability and cost;
3. inspect failures, incidents, overrides, support demand and excluded cases;
4. compare baseline/alternatives;
5. examine evidence limitations and workforce/customer effects;
6. record decision, conditions, owner, and next gate.

**Outputs:** decision memorandum and updated portfolio record.

## 4. Interview guides

### Executive sponsor

- Which strategic result is currently constrained?
- What would be materially different if the constraint were removed?
- Which trade-offs and risks are acceptable or unacceptable?
- What existing AI activity is strategic, experimental, duplicated, or unknown?
- Who owns the outcome and can change cross-functional work?
- What evidence would justify investment or stopping?
- How will workforce and affected-person impacts be governed?

### Workflow owner / manager

- What triggers the workflow and how does it end?
- Which cases consume disproportionate time or judgment?
- Where does work wait, loop, rekey, duplicate, or fail?
- Which decisions are delegated, escalated, or ambiguous?
- Which policies/sources are authoritative, and where do they conflict?
- What hidden work do users perform?
- What help/support exists today?
- What could go seriously wrong if an answer or action is wrong?

### Intended user

- Walk me through the last routine and difficult case.
- What do you open, search, copy, calculate, decide, and record?
- What do you know that is not in the official process?
- Where are you uncertain and who do you ask?
- What mistakes are easy to make or hard to detect?
- What would make assistance useful, annoying, unsafe, or untrustworthy?
- When must you retain control or seek a second decision?
- How should help, correction, or complaint work?

### Technical/data owner

- Which system owns each fact and state?
- What access, API, event, identity, latency, quality, and retention constraints exist?
- Which integrations/actions are reversible, idempotent, and verifiable?
- What fails, retries, drifts, or changes without notice?
- What approved AI/model/platform patterns exist?
- What evidence is available for monitoring, cost, incidents, and rollback?

### Legal/privacy/security/risk

- What purpose, people, decision/action, data, model/provider, and jurisdiction context matters?
- What is prohibited or high-consequence?
- Which assessments, records, transparency, oversight, rights, or approvals are required?
- Which threat/misuse/incident scenarios must be tested?
- Who accepts residual risk and under what evidence?
- What changes require reassessment?

## 6. Board memorandum template

### Decision requested

State one explicit decision: fund discovery, approve pilot, expand scope, accept condition, pause, or retire.

### Outcome and boundary

- strategic/business outcome;
- workflow start/end;
- owner, users, affected people;
- current maturity and authorised scope.

### Evidence

| Dimension | Baseline/current | Observed result | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Outcome |  |  |  |
| Quality |  |  |  |
| Risk/control |  |  |  |
| Human work |  |  |  |
| Adoption/support |  |  |  |
| Reliability/cost |  |  |  |

### Material failures and unresolved issues

Include incidents, unsafe/incorrect cases, overrides, support burden, excluded populations, vendor or control dependencies, and evidence contamination.

### Options

1. expand with conditions;
2. revise/retest;
3. pause for dependency/evidence;
4. stop/retire;
5. alternative non-AI/process investment.

### Recommendation and conditions

Name owner, resources, limits, next evidence gate, review trigger, and stop condition.

## 7. Employee communication template

### What is changing

Describe the workflow and intended outcome in plain language.

### What the AI system does

List exact tasks such as finding approved information, proposing a category, or preparing a draft.

### What it does not do

List prohibited/out-of-scope decisions and actions.

### Your authority and responsibility

Explain what the employee must check, decide, correct, reject, record, or escalate.

### Information and monitoring

Explain approved data, prohibited data, what is logged/observed, why, access/retention, and employee/privacy information as required.

### Help, concern, incident, and appeal

Provide visible channels and state that appropriate escalation will not be penalised.

### How the organisation will learn

Explain which quality, workload, adoption, support, risk, and outcome effects are being evaluated and how changes will be communicated.

## 8. Executive and employee FAQ prompts

### Executive FAQ

- Why this workflow rather than another?
- Why AI rather than conventional improvement?
- What does the evidence prove and not prove?
- Who is accountable when the system is wrong?
- Which actions can it take?
- What is the legal/risk classification status?
- What is the production/support cost and vendor dependency?
- What would cause us to stop?

### Employee FAQ

- Is use optional, expected, or required for this workflow?
- Will this affect my role, workload, or performance evaluation?
- Can my use/content be seen and by whom?
- What information may I enter?
- What if I disagree with the output?
- How do I report a harmful or incorrect result?
- What happens when the system is unavailable?
- Who maintains it and how will changes be communicated?

Never publish a reassuring answer the organisation cannot substantiate.

## 9. Workshop quality check

After each session confirm:

- decisions and non-decisions are explicit;
- owners have authority and accepted the record;
- assumptions are labelled;
- current and future state were not mixed;
- dissent and affected-person concerns were retained;
- sensitive information is handled correctly;
- evidence and limitations are linked;
- actions specify owner, output, and decision they enable;
- public/client claims remain unapproved unless explicitly authorised;
- the next gate can result in stop.
