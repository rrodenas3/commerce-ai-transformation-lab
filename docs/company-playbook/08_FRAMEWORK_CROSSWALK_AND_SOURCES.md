# Framework Crosswalk and Research Sources

## How the playbook is grounded

The Company AI Transformation Playbook is an original synthesis. External frameworks and examples inform its coverage; they do not make the playbook equivalent to those frameworks or transfer any certification, compliance, adoption, performance, or value claim.

## 1. Integrated lifecycle crosswalk

| Playbook decision stage | NIST AI RMF reference | ISO/IEC 42001 management-system lens | EU AI Act / legal workstream | OECD lens | Operational output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prepare and mandate | Govern | organisational context, leadership, planning | establish provider/deployer/use context; applicable obligations need counsel | economic context; actors | mandate, owners, policy/evidence boundary |
| Observe current work | Map | planning and operational context | affected use context, people, data, decisions/actions | people/planet; data/input; task/output | workflow, authority, information and impact map |
| Qualify/select | Map + Govern | risk/opportunity planning | prohibited/high-risk/transparency and other applicability screening | classification dimensions | portfolio path and lighthouse decision |
| Design workflow/authority | Map + Manage | operational planning/control | human oversight, information, transparency and other context-specific requirements | lifecycle actors and task/output | future workflow, decision rights, controls |
| Architecture/vendor | Govern + Map + Manage | support and operation | value-chain role, technical/record/control obligations as applicable | data/model/system operator | architecture, connection and vendor decisions |
| Freeze evaluation | Measure | performance evaluation | required quality/robustness/records/oversight evidence as applicable | system characteristics and impacts | evaluation contract, cases, thresholds, claims |
| Build and test | Measure + Manage | Do/Check | implement and test applicable controls | model/task/deployment context | tested bounded slice and failure evidence |
| Enable and pilot | Govern + Manage | competence, awareness, operational control | Article 4 literacy; transparency/oversight and other applicable duties | users/operators/affected stakeholders | role capability, support, pilot observations |
| Operate and monitor | Measure + Manage | Check/Act | monitoring, incident/record obligations as applicable | ongoing lifecycle/accountability | operational quality, risk, adoption, reliability, cost, outcome |
| Expand/change/retire | all functions iteratively | continual improvement | reassess changed purpose/context/system and applicable duties | changed classification context | controlled change, new gate, or decommission |

The crosswalk is a navigation aid. Use the actual framework texts, official guidance, applicable law, and qualified specialists for formal work.

## 2. NIST AI RMF crosswalk

NIST describes Govern as cross-cutting and the overall process as iterative across the AI lifecycle. It explicitly includes business context, risk tolerance, inventory, roles, human-AI responsibilities, proficiency, independent review, test/evaluation/verification/validation, monitoring, third-party risk, feedback, and proceed/stop decisions. [NIST AI RMF Core](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/)

| NIST function | Playbook implementation |
| --- | --- |
| Govern | board/executive accountability, ATO/governance topology, policy, inventory, ownership, literacy, vendor/change/incident/evidence systems |
| Map | outcome/workflow/people/authority/information/AI/context ontology, use-case qualification, affected-person and impact mapping |
| Measure | preregistered case/evaluation system across component, control, workflow, human, operational and outcome layers |
| Manage | controls, response/recovery, investment gates, pilot/operation, residual-risk decisions, change and retirement |

The NIST Generative AI Profile adds cross-sector considerations for generative AI and should be used directly when relevant. [NIST AI 600-1 — Generative AI Profile](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence)

## 3. ISO/IEC 42001 crosswalk

ISO describes ISO/IEC 42001:2023 as the first AI management-system standard and as a structured approach for organisations developing, providing, or using AI systems to manage risks and opportunities and continually improve. [ISO/IEC 42001 overview](https://www.iso.org/standard/42001) and [ISO AI management systems](https://www.iso.org/artificial-intelligence/ai-management-systems)

| PDCA idea | Playbook implementation |
| --- | --- |
| Plan | mandate, context, outcomes, inventory, portfolio, risk, roles, workflow/architecture/evaluation design |
| Do | bounded build, connections, controls, enablement, pilot and operation |
| Check | evaluation, human observation, monitoring, audit/assurance, outcome/adoption/risk review |
| Act | corrective action, controlled change, expand/revise/pause/stop, decommission, reusable learning |

The playbook has not been assessed against every requirement of ISO/IEC 42001 and must not be represented as an implementation or certification package without formal gap assessment and authorised work.

Relevant ISO catalogue entries include:

- [ISO/IEC 22989 — AI concepts and terminology](https://www.iso.org/sectors/it-technologies/ai)
- [ISO/IEC 23894 — AI risk management guidance](https://www.iso.org/sectors/it-technologies/ai)
- [ISO/IEC 42001 — AI management system](https://www.iso.org/standard/42001)
- [ISO/IEC 42005 — AI system impact assessment](https://www.iso.org/sectors/it-technologies/ai)
- [ISO/IEC 38507 — governance implications of organisational AI use](https://www.iso.org/committee/6794475/x/catalogue/)

Availability, editions, and applicability should be checked at time of use.

## 4. EU AI Act workstream

The Commission's official AI Act page, updated in August 2026, describes a risk-based legal framework and its application timeline. It states that the Act became generally applicable on 2 August 2026 with specified exceptions and later high-risk dates. It also describes transparency rules, AI-literacy measures, governance/enforcement, and supporting guidance. [European Commission — AI Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)

The playbook operationalises questions that help a qualified company team begin analysis:

- What system/use exists and who is provider, deployer, operator, user, or affected person?
- What purpose, task, decision/action, population, data, model, context, and geography are involved?
- Could a prohibited, high-risk, transparency, general-purpose-model, or other regulated context apply?
- What AI-literacy, transparency, information, human-oversight, documentation, record, monitoring, incident, or assurance measures may be required?
- What changes would alter that analysis?

It does not answer the legal questions.

Current official references:

- [AI Act overview and timeline](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)
- [AI literacy — Article 4 Q&A](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/ai-literacy-questions-answers)
- [Article 50 transparency guidelines](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/guidelines-transparency-obligations-providers-and-deployers-ai-systems)
- [AI Act Single Information Platform](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/)
- [Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)

Legal/regulatory content is time-sensitive. Recheck official sources and obtain qualified advice before company use.

## 5. OECD classification and accountability

The OECD framework distinguishes applied AI systems across people/planet, economic context, data/input, model, and task/output dimensions. This supports the playbook's insistence that the same model can require different governance in different use contexts. [OECD AI classification framework](https://oecd.ai/en/classification)

The OECD accountability principle connects lifecycle roles, traceability, ongoing risk management, and cooperation among AI actors and stakeholders. [OECD AI accountability principle](https://oecd.ai/en/dashboards/ai-principles/P9?s=03)

Playbook use:

- classify context before selecting generic controls;
- include users and affected people, not only developers;
- trace data, process, decisions, outputs, actions, and change;
- allocate accountability according to role and ability to act.

## 6. OWASP security references

Use OWASP guidance directly for threat modelling and technical verification. The playbook adds workflow/authority/evidence context around those controls.

- [OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-for-2026/)
- [OWASP GenAI Security Project](https://genai.owasp.org/)

Playbook areas informed:

- prompt/document injection;
- sensitive information and secrets;
- improper output/tool handling;
- excessive agency and tool permissions;
- memory/knowledge poisoning;
- insecure inter-agent/system communication;
- supply-chain and vendor risk;
- monitoring, containment, recovery, and incident evidence.

## 7. Transformation and enablement operating guidance

OpenAI Academy's Agent Activator guidance uses a Sponsor, Deploy, and Reimagine contribution model and defines workflow ownership through design, development, operationalisation, support, measurement, maintenance, and ongoing outcome. It also distinguishes learning participation from demonstrated practice. [Getting Started as an Agent Activator](https://academy.openai.com/en/public/clubs/champions-ecqup/resources/getting-started-as-an-ai-activator-2026-06-08)

The playbook adapts those ideas into a vendor-neutral role topology:

- executive sponsor;
- AI transformation director/office;
- workflow transformation lead;
- AI enablement/adoption lead;
- workflow owner/Activator;
- platform/data/architecture/control partners;
- managers, intended users, and affected-person routes.

## 11. Source-use rules

1. Prefer official regulation, standards bodies, framework publishers, technical documentation, and first-party company evidence.
2. Distinguish normative requirements, voluntary frameworks, guidance, company claims, and analyst inference.
3. Check time-sensitive sources at the point of company use.
4. Link to the exact supporting page and record access/version where material.
5. Do not copy another organisation's metrics into a baseline, target, forecast, or business case.
6. Do not imply endorsement by cited organisations.
7. Do not describe framework alignment as certification or compliance.
8. Record contradictory or uncertain evidence.
9. Separate evidence used to design from evidence produced by the engagement.
10. Retain only sources that affect a decision, requirement, risk, test, enablement need, or claim.
